The gut is five metres, two exits, and four clocks.

A fact-audit and design brief for the gastrointestinal mode — built from the 2026-10-30 deep-research report. Every number below carries the status the report gave it and the source it came from. Where the report hedged, this page hedges. Where it could not verify, this page says so.

Design verdict

Build the route at in-vivo scale — ~5 m mouth-to-anus (Helander & Fändriks 2014), not the cadaveric ~9 m — with a pH gradient, three nested surface-amplification scales, and two different exit routes from the villus as the core mechanic.

First case: coeliac disease (villous-atrophy toggle). Second, short episode: H. pylori peptic ulcer. The portal-vs-lacteal split already exists in the game's lymph mode — the GI mode should be the mode that explains it.

02 — THE ROUTE

Station by station

Pick a station. Transit time is the curriculum: seconds in the oesophagus, hours in the stomach and small bowel, a day or two in the colon. Chyme does not travel at uniform speed.

03 — SURFACE & MICROSCOPY

How “tennis court” happened — and why it fell

Textbooks multiplied three idealised factors — folds ×3, villi ×10, microvilli ×20 = ×600 — then multiplied by a smooth-cylinder area based on a cadaveric length, yielding 200–300 m². Helander & Fändriks (2014) instead used the in-vivo length and measured biopsies: plicae amplify only ~×1.6, villi + microvilli together ×60–120 for small intestine, total ~32 m².

The textbook multiplication Reality-check only

×3 folds × ×10 villi × ×20 microvilli = ×600

The ×600 chain is not fabricated — each factor appears in histology texts — but multiplying maxima from different preparations overstates the real, distension-dependent area. Show it in the L4 Reality-check, side by side with the measured estimate of ~30–40 m².

The measured scales Established

  • Villi: 0.5–1.6 mm tall, 10–40 per mm², millions total; tallest in jejunum, shorter in ileum, absent in colon.
  • Microvilli: ~1 µm long, 0.1 µm wide; ~1,000–3,000 per enterocyte. Range debated
  • Plicae circulares: prominent in jejunum, fading in ileum; measured amplification ~×1.6 (Helander morphometry) vs ×3 textbook. Debated

The crypt–villus conveyor

Lgr5+ stem cells sit at the crypt base, intermingled with Paneth cells. Transit-amplifying daughters divide, differentiate (enterocytes, goblet, enteroendocrine, tuft cells; Paneth cells migrate down and live weeks), migrate up the villus, and are shed at the tip after 3–5 days — some human texts say 5–7 days. One of the fastest self-renewals in the body, and the mechanism behind both acid survival (§05) and coeliac crypt hyperplasia (§09).

Enterocyte detail: tall columnar, basal nucleus, apical brush border under a glycocalyx — a fuzzy glycoprotein coat holding the brush-border enzymes (lactase, sucrase-isomaltase, peptidases) that finish digestion at the surface. Tight junctions apically are a regulated leak, not a weld; jejunal junctions are leakier than colonic.

Enteroendocrine cells: ~1% of the epithelium, >20 hormones — the largest endocrine organ by diffuse spread. Cast: G (gastrin), I (CCK), S (secretin), K (GIP), L (GLP-1, PYY), M (motilin), D (somatostatin), enterochromaffin (serotonin), ECL (histamine). Modern single-cell work shows cells can co-express and switch hormones — the one-cell-one-hormone letter code is a useful Simplification, not literal.

Paneth cells: crypt base, long-lived, granules of α-defensins (HD5/6 in humans) and lysozyme, plus stem-cell niche signals (Wnt3, EGF, Notch ligand). Dysfunction is linked to Crohn's ileitis — a forward hook for the IBD arc.

Sources: Barker/Clevers Lgr5 work as summarised in Nat Commun 2020;11:1936 (migration 3–5 days); “Enteroendocrine Dynamics,” Endocr Rev 2020;41:bnaa018.

04 — INSIDE ONE VILLUS

Two exits, two destinations

Each villus holds a capillary network just under the epithelium and a blind-ending central lacteal. Which drain a nutrient takes is decided mostly by size and solubility. Tap a nutrient to see its route.

→ Villus capillary → hepatic portal vein → liver first

Small, water-soluble cargo. The liver screens and processes it before it reaches the rest of the body.

→ Lacteal → mesenteric lymph → cisterna chyli → thoracic duct → systemic blood

Chylomicrons (~75–1,200 nm) are too large for capillary endothelium; lacteals have open junctions. This route bypasses liver first pass — it is exactly the chylomicron traffic already shown in the game's cisterna chyli.

Transporters worth naming

  • SGLT1 — apical, 2 Na⁺ : 1 glucose/galactose, secondary active. The basis of oral rehydration solution.
  • GLUT5 — fructose, facilitated, capacity-limited. GLUT2 — basolateral exit.
  • PepT1 — H⁺-coupled di/tripeptide uptake; a major protein route, often omitted.
  • DMT1 — iron, mainly duodenum/proximal jejunum, after Fe³⁺ reduction (Dcytb; vitamin C helps). Export via ferroportin, blocked by hepatic hepcidin. There is no regulated iron excretion — absorption is the control point.
  • Cubam (cubilin–amnionless) — terminal ileum; takes up the B12–intrinsic-factor complex. Liver stores 2–5 mg of B12 — years' supply — so deficiency is slow.
  • ASBT — terminal ileum; bile-acid reuptake (see pool, right).

Fat, step by step Established

Emulsification (bile salts + motility) → pancreatic lipase/colipase → mixed micelles ferry fatty acids and monoglycerides across the unstirred water layer → enterocyte re-esterifies them in smooth ER → chylomicron assembly with apoB-48 in the Golgi → exocytosis → lacteal. Bile salts themselves are not absorbed here — they ride on to the ileum.

The bile-acid loop

Pool ~3 g (2–4 g), recycled ~4–12 cycles/day (many texts simplify to 6–10), secreting 12–36 g/day; ≥95% reabsorbed in the terminal ileum; 0.2–0.6 g/day lost in faeces equals hepatic synthesis from cholesterol. Ileal resection or disease → bile-acid diarrhoea + fat malabsorption: a perfect portal/lymph cross-mode quiz.

05 — CLOCKS & MOTILITY

Four clocks, nested honestly

Interstitial cells of Cajal are the pacemakers. The slow wave sets the maximum contraction frequency; nerves and hormones decide whether a contraction actually happens. The frequency gradient — fast proximally, slow distally — is why contents move aborally.

Slow-wave frequency by region Established

Duodenal 12/min ≈ breathing rate (12–16/min) is a nice coincidence to teach — not a coupling to imply. Heartbeat ~1/s and breathing are the fast foreground; gastric 3/min is a visible 20-second swell — playable in real time. The MMC (90–120 min) and colonic transit (24–48 h) belong on a mission elapsed-time HUD, with time-lapse ×60 / ×1,000 clearly labelled.

Gastric emptying: liquids are not solids

Solids wait while the antrum grinds them to 1–2 mm; only then does the pylorus pass them, in spurts. Fat, hyperosmolality and acid in the duodenum slow emptying via CCK, secretin and the enterogastric reflex. A single “2–4 h” figure for everything is wrong. Source: Farrell 2019, gastric emptying scintigraphy.

Patterns of movement

  • Peristalsis — contraction behind, receptive relaxation ahead; the propulsive wave.
  • Segmentation — stationary ring contractions; mixing, little net movement. The dominant fed pattern in small bowel. Haustral churning is the colon analogue.
  • Migrating motor complex — fasting only, cycle 90–120 min: Phase I quiescence → II irregular → III, ~5–10 min of maximal rhythmic contractions sweeping stomach→ileum — the “housekeeper” that clears debris, shed cells and bacteria → IV transition. Motilin peaks initiate gastric Phase III; feeding abolishes the MMC instantly. Large indigestible objects wait for Phase III, when the pylorus opens wider.
  • Gastric accommodation — swallow triggers vagovagal receptive relaxation (NO/VIP) of the fundus; the stomach holds ~1–1.5 L with little pressure rise. Failure → early satiety.
  • Gastrocolic reflex — food in the stomach → colonic mass movements (1–3+/day, often post-breakfast) and defaecation urge. The effect is real; the relative contributions of gastrin/CCK vs extrinsic nerves are debated in detail.
06 — ACID & DEFENCE

Attack vs defence — the balance is the lesson

Parietal secretion is ~150–155 mmol/L HCl at pH ~0.8–0.9 before mixing; the fed/fasting lumen sits at pH 1.5–3.5. “Stomach pH = 1,” constant, is not a fact.

The parietal cell Established

Apical H⁺/K⁺-ATPase — the proton pump, target of PPIs — is inserted from tubulovesicles on activation; Cl⁻ follows; HCO₃⁻ exits basolaterally (the alkaline tide). Three stimulants converge and potentiate each other: histamine (ECL cells, H2, cAMP — the dominant amplifier), gastrin (G cells, acting largely via ECL histamine), acetylcholine (vagus, M3, Ca²⁺). Somatostatin is the main brake. Total parietal cells ~1×10⁹ is a widely repeated order-of-magnitude estimate. Debated

Phases — give ranges, not a pie chart: cephalic ~20–30% (sight/smell/thought, vagal), gastric ~50–70% (distension + peptides → gastrin), intestinal ~5–10% stimulatory, rapidly overridden by intestinal inhibitory signals. Textbooks vary (30/60/10 vs 20/70/5). Debated in detail

Why the stomach does not digest itself

No single answer — it is multi-layer:

  • Mucus–bicarbonate barrier: adherent mucus gel traps HCO₃⁻; surface pH ~6–7 while the lumen is ~2 — a gradient within ~100–500 µm.
  • Tight junctions + rapid restitution of surface epithelium.
  • Prostaglandins (PGE₂/PGI₂) maintain mucus, HCO₃⁻ and mucosal blood flow.
  • Rich blood flow washes out back-diffused H⁺.
  • Epithelial renewal every few days; pepsinogen stays inactive until luminal acid activates it.

H. pylori — lives in the mucus, not the acid Established

A spiral, flagellated, microaerophilic Gram-negative in the mucus layer. Its urease splits urea into an NH₃/CO₂ cloud that neutralises its microenvironment. Adhesins bind the epithelium; VacA/CagA (strain-dependent) plus inflammation disrupt the barrier and alter gastrin/somatostatin. Antral-predominant infection tends to raise acid → duodenal ulcer pattern; corpus pangastritis tends to lower acid → gastric ulcer and cancer risk. Most infected people never get an ulcer — do not show infection as inevitable ulcer.

NSAIDs — defence lowered, acid unchanged Established

Systemic COX-1 inhibition → fewer prostaglandins → less mucus, less HCO₃⁻, less mucosal blood flow is the main mechanism, plus direct topical irritation. Acid need not be high for injury. That asymmetry — attack vs defence — is the teachable model for the ulcer case.

And the correction to the school version: acid denatures protein, activates pepsinogen, kills many microbes and aids iron/B12 handling — but most chemical digestion is pancreatic and brush-border work in the small intestine. Acid does not dissolve bone or metal on meal timescales, and many pathogens and spores survive it. “Acid melts everything” — wrong

07 — MICROBIOME AT REAL SCALE

Geography is the lesson: eight orders of magnitude

Density rises ~8 orders of magnitude along the route. The small bowel is sparsely colonised — acid, bile, fast transit and the MMC housekeeper keep it that way. The colon is a fermentation vat. Do not fill the jejunum with a dense bacterial swarm.

Bacterial density by segment (per gram of content, log scale)

Totals: 3.8×10¹³ bacteria vs 3.0×10¹³ human cells in a 70-kg reference man, ratio ~1.3:1, bacterial mass ~0.2 kg (Sender, Fuchs & Milo 2016). The 10:1 figure traces to a 1970s back-of-envelope estimate. Delightful Reality-check from the same paper: defaecation can transiently flip the ratio.

The colon's two mucus layers

Johansson, Hansson and colleagues (PNAS 2008, 2011): the inner MUC2 layer is dense, stratified and essentially bacteria-free; the outer layer is expanded MUC2 and is the habitat. Caveat the report insists on: those thicknesses are mouse measurements; human layers exist but figures vary — do not print mouse µm values as human. The small intestine has a single, looser, penetrable mucus layer — do not copy the colon bilayer everywhere.

Fermentation & SCFAs Established

Fibre fermentation in the colon yields acetate, propionate and butyrate — the preferred fuel of colonocytes, oxidised locally, supporting the barrier with anti-inflammatory signalling. That colonocytes are “starved” without butyrate is an emerging mechanistic story — present it as debated detail beyond the fuel fact.

Dominant colon phyla: Firmicutes (now Bacillota) and Bacteroidetes (Bacteroidota) together form the large majority, with Actinobacteria, Proteobacteria and Verrucomicrobia minorities. Phylum names are being renamed — the Codex can note both.

Show: a density gradient, an anaerobe-dominated colon lumen held off the epithelium by inner mucus, fermentation and SCFA pickup in the colon only. Don't show: bacteria inside cells or tissue in health, uniform “good bacteria” characters, or one-species-fixes-everything probiotic claims.

Sterile at birth? Debated, leaning defensible — Perez-Muñoz et al. 2017 found evidence for a placental/fetal microbiome weak and largely contamination in low-biomass sequencing. Present the healthy fetal gut as sterile/near-sterile; colonisation happens during and after birth. Do not teach a placental microbiome as fact.

08 — ENTERIC NERVOUS SYSTEM & GUT–BRAIN

Autonomous, yes. A second thinking brain, no.

Two plexuses, one reflex

  • Myenteric (Auerbach) — between longitudinal and circular muscle; motility.
  • Submucosal (Meissner) — secretion, blood flow, sensing. Plus enteric glia, more numerous than the neurons.
  • Peristaltic reflex: mucosal stimulus (distension; 5-HT from enterochromaffin cells) → intrinsic primary afferent neurons → ascending excitatory motor neurons (ACh, substance P — contraction on the oral side) + descending inhibitory (NO, VIP, ATP — relaxation on the anal side).
  • It runs without the CNS — isolated gut peristalses. “Second brain” is a metaphor for autonomy, not cognition. Simplification if literal
  • Neuron count: hundreds of millions; estimates range ~100–500 million depending on counting method and species extrapolation. Anyone giving one exact number is overselling. Debated

Serotonin: the correction that matters

~90% (often quoted 90–95%) of the body's serotonin is made by gut enterochromaffin cells (TPH1). It drives motility, secretion and nausea signalling via vagal and ENS 5-HT₃/5-HT₄ receptors; platelets mop up the excess.

It does not cross the blood–brain barrier. Brain serotonin is made separately (TPH2). So “90% of your serotonin is in your gut, therefore gut health directly sets mood serotonin” is Simplification-wrong. Gut–brain influence on mood is real but indirect — vagal, immune, metabolite, tryptophan availability — and effect sizes are debated.

The vagus mostly listens: ~80% of its gut fibres are afferent — distension, nutrients (via CCK/GLP-1/PYY signalling to vagal terminals), nausea — while it modulates upper-GI motility and acid. It does not micromanage the colon; pelvic nerves serve the distal colon and rectum. A good in-game “radio link to HQ” UI, provided the Codex notes ENS local autonomy.

Duodenal inputs — bile & pancreas Established

Bile ~0.5 L/day (some texts up to 1.0 L — present the range 0.5–1.0 L). The gallbladder stores and concentrates it between meals. Bile contains no enzymes — its jobs are emulsification, micelles, and excretion of bilirubin and cholesterol.

Pancreatic juice 1.0–1.5 L/day, pH ~8.0–8.3. Duct cells make the HCO₃⁻-rich fluid (secretin-driven, triggered by acid); acinar cells make the enzyme-rich secretion (CCK-driven, triggered by fatty acids/peptides): lipase/colipase, amylase, and proteases shipped as zymogens — trypsinogen is activated by enteropeptidase at the brush border, and trypsin activates the rest. A safety cascade worth its own Codex card.

Sphincter of Oddi: tonically closed between meals, diverting bile to the gallbladder; CCK relaxes it while contracting the gallbladder. One hormone, two opposite coordinated actions — the teachable point.

09 — BEDSIDE, BEFORE YOU FLY

Sounds are a clue. They never prove.

The exam sequence is inspection → auscultation → percussion → palpation — auscultate before palpating so you don't stir up sounds. (StatPearls, Abdominal Examination, NBK459220.)

Bowel sounds — with the honesty flag

Normal: 5–35 per minute (StatPearls; 5–34 in nursing texts), irregular low-pitched gurgling and clicking from gas + fluid — synthesisable as irregular filtered gurgles at random intervals, not a metronome. Hypoactive: soft/infrequent (ileus, peritonitis, post-op). Hyperactive: loud, frequent, high-pitched tinkling and rushes with colicky pain in early obstruction. “Absent” only after prolonged listening — texts vary 1–5 minutes, which is itself a tell.

Poor standalone value Critical reviews find poor inter-observer agreement, wide normal variation, sounds that transmit across the abdomen (not localised), and low sensitivity/specificity alone — hyperactive sounds for obstruction are quoted at sensitivity only ~40%, specificity ~89–94% in secondary sources. Presence of sounds does not exclude obstruction or ileus; absence does not prove it. Rounds should reward: sounds support; history and imaging decide.

The rest of the exam

  • Succussion splash — splashing on rocking a fluid+gas-filled stomach; abnormal only >3–4 h after a meal/drink → gastric outlet obstruction. Normal shortly after drinking; the timing caveat is the exam point.
  • Percussion — generalised tympany over gas-filled bowel; dull over liver, solid organs, full bladder.
  • Shifting dullness — flank dullness supine that moves to the dependent side on rolling → free fluid/ascites; needs roughly >500 mL–1 L to detect reliably, sensitivity modest. Direct link to the liver/portal mode.
  • Murphy sign — fingers under the right costal margin; deep inspiration brings the inflamed gallbladder down onto them → inspiratory arrest → acute cholecystitis. Sonographic Murphy is more reliable than clinical.
  • Palpation — tender area last; guarding, rigidity, rebound = peritonism. Inspection hooks: distension, visible peristalsis, scars, herniae.

Outside views: tests & the real “inside view”

FIT (faecal immunochemical test, detects human globin; no dietary restriction) is the modern colorectal screening triage — it has replaced guaiac FOBT. Terminology revised Faecal calprotectin (neutrophil protein, common cut-off ~50 µg/g) separates IBD inflammation from IBS and rules IBD out well — it is not a cancer test, and rises with infection and NSAIDs. Anti-tTG IgA (+ total IgA) is the coeliac test (§09 case).

Endoscopy sees the mucosal surface at centimetre–millimetre scale in real colour — redness, ulcers, polyps, bleeding, flattened duodenal folds and scalloping in coeliac — plus biopsy forceps for the microscopic scale. It does not show individual villi in normal view, and never shows microvilli, cells, transporters or bacteria. The micro-sub is therefore three scale-jumps beyond endoscopy: lumen (endoscopy-true) → villus forest (histology scale) → brush border/cell (EM scale). Label the scale on screen at all times — the single best anti-misconception UI feature.

First case: coeliac disease

One toggle teaches the whole mode. Drag the Marsh stage and watch the villus forest — then note what fails downstream: duodenal iron pickup, fat pickup into the lacteal (pale, scanty chyle — a direct callback to lymph mode), and anaemia in the circulation mode.

Marsh 0123a3b3c

Slider steps: 0 · 1 · 2 · 3a/3b combined view · 3c. Crypt depth grows as villi shrink — compensation, drawn to the report's ratios.

Marsh 0 — normal

Villus : crypt ratio: 3–5 : 1

Serology: anti-tTG IgA — always check total IgA, since IgA deficiency causes false negatives — plus EMA. HLA-DQ2/DQ8 is essentially required genetically but common in the population, so it excludes rather than confirms. Builder simplifications from the report

Villi = one instanced finger mesh with height/density parameters per segment — the coeliac toggle is that one parameter. Brush border = fresnel/rim fuzz shader + glycocalyx noise, never geometry. Plicae = torus-segment instancing fading through the ileum. Bacteria = density-graded particle field, excluded from the colon's inner-mucus shell by a distance field. Chylomicrons = large instanced spheres in the lacteal only; sugars/amino acids = small particles in the capillary — the size difference is the lesson. Slow waves = a travelling emissive band on the wall shader at the region-correct frequency.
