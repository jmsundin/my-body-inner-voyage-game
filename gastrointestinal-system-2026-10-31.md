**Verdict up front.** The gut is the game's best "two exits" system: almost everything absorbed leaves by blood (portal vein → liver), but long-chain fat leaves by lymph (lacteal → the chylomicrons already in the lymph mode). That single fork, taught at one villus, justifies the whole mode. It is also the system where the classic textbook numbers are most wrong — gut length, surface area, and bacterial counts have all been revised downward by direct measurement, and the corrected numbers are *more* interesting than the myths.

**Headline corrected numbers.** Living gut ~5 m mouth-to-anus, not 7–9 m (cadaveric). Total digestive surface ~32 m² (small intestine ~30, colon ~2) — "half a badminton court," not a tennis court. Bacteria ~3.8×10¹³ vs human cells ~3.0×10¹³ — roughly 1:1, not 10:1. Daily fluid load ~9 L, of which only 100–200 mL leaves in stool. Small-bowel transit 3–5 h; colon 24–48 h. Slow waves: stomach 3/min, duodenum 11–12/min, ileum 8–9/min.

**Recommended first case: coeliac disease. Short second episode: H. pylori peptic ulcer.** Reasoning in §5.

**[Revised] Do not build the route by summing textbook segment lengths.** Duodenum 25 cm, jejunum ~2.5 m, ileum ~3.5 m, colon 1.5 m are cadaveric-framing figures; the living oro-anal total measured in vivo is ~5 m because smooth-muscle tone shortens the living gut (Helander & Fändriks, *Scand J Gastroenterol* 2014, DOI 10.3109/00365521.2014.898326). **Warp exits at G5/G6 (the core mechanic):** at any villus the player chooses — follow a glucose molecule into the villus capillary → portal vein → liver (future liver mode), or follow a chylomicron into the lacteal → join the *existing* lymph route at the cisterna chyli the player already knows.

## 2. What it looks like, station by station

**Mouth/oesophagus.** Stratified squamous lining (built for abrasion, not absorption); oesophageal peristalsis is a travelling squeeze the sub rides *ahead of*, not a slide. *Microscopy:* squamous epithelium histology; endoscopy shows pale pink squamous mucosa and the Z-line at the gastro-oesophageal junction.

**Stomach.** Rugae (folds that flatten as it fills), gastric pits opening to glands: parietal cells (acid, intrinsic factor), chief cells (pepsinogen), G cells (gastrin, antrum), surface mucous cells. Parietal secretion is ~150–155 mmol/L HCl (pH ~0.9 as secreted; lumen pH 1.5–3.5 after mixing); parietal cell count ~1 billion **[Debated estimate — quote as an estimate]**. *Microscopy:* H&E gland layers; parietal cells large, pink, with intracellular canaliculi on EM. **[Wrong]** The stomach is not where digestion mainly happens chemically for most nutrients and it absorbs essentially no nutrients (water, some alcohol/aspirin-class drugs excepted) — it is a mixer, steriliser and metering gate.

**Small intestine — the amplification stack, corrected.** **[Revised]** Plicae circulares ×~1.6 (measured, not ×3), villi and microvilli together ×60–120 — the classic ×600 comes from multiplying three textbook maxima (×3 × ×10 × ×20). Total ~32 m² (Helander & Fändriks 2014). Villus: finger/leaf-like, ~0.5–1 mm tall, enterocytes with brush border (microvilli ~1 µm, glycocalyx coat), goblet cells interspersed, crypt of Lieberkühn at the base with Lgr5+ stem cells; cells migrate crypt→villus tip and are shed in ~3–5 days — the gut lining the player flies past is days old, a lovely Codex beat. Paneth cells at the crypt base secrete defensins. Enteroendocrine cells are scattered single cells (I cells → CCK, S cells → secretin, K → GIP, L → GLP-1, enterochromaffin → serotonin). *Microscopy:* LM villus/crypt architecture; EM brush border and tight junctions; SEM villus fields.

**Villus interior.** One central lacteal (blind lymph capillary) wrapped by a capillary network, smooth-muscle strand that pumps the villus. This cross-section is the zoom station: blood capillaries take the water-soluble traffic, the lacteal takes fat (§3).

**Ileum.** Peyer's patches (aggregated lymphoid follicles) under M cells that sample luminal antigen — a direct visual link to the lymph mode's follicles. Fewer/shorter villi than jejunum.

**Colon.** No villi — flat surface with straight crypts; dense goblet cells; haustra. **Mucus bilayer [Established in mouse, human thicknesses unverified]:** inner MUC2 layer essentially bacteria-free, outer layer colonised (Johansson et al., *PNAS* 2008/2011). Bacteria at real density (§6) fill the lumen centre, *not* the wall surface — draw a clear inner mucus moat or the image lies.

## 3. What moves and on what clock

**New clock: the slow wave.** Interstitial cells of Cajal pace electrical slow waves — stomach ~3/min, duodenum ~11–12/min, ileum ~8–9/min — setting the *maximum* contraction rhythm; contractions happen when spike potentials ride the wave. Peristalsis (propulsive, ENS-coordinated) vs segmentation (mixing, dominant in fed small bowel). Coupling: the slow-wave clock is independent of the heartbeat, but the **migrating motor complex** gives a session-level rhythm — fasting cycle 90–120 min, phase III the "housekeeper" sweep (motilin-associated) that clears debris between meals and stops on eating (Deloose et al., *Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol* 2012). Gastric accommodation (vagovagal, NO/VIP) lets the fundus relax to receive a meal; the gastrocolic reflex drives colonic mass movements after meals. Game proposal: slow wave as the per-station tempo (visible wall rhythm, different at G3 vs G4 vs G6 — the player *hears/feels* the frequency change), MMC phase III as an occasional dramatic sweep event in fasting mode.

**Absorption traffic (what moves where):** glucose/galactose via SGLT1, fructose via GLUT5, exit via GLUT2 → portal blood. Long-chain fat: bile-salt micelles → enterocyte → re-esterified → chylomicrons (apoB-48) → lacteal → lymph. Vitamin B12–intrinsic factor via cubam receptors, *terminal ileum only*. Iron via DMT1/ferroportin in the duodenum, gated by hepcidin. Bile acids: pool ~3 g, cycles 4–12×/day, ≥95% reabsorbed in terminal ileum (ASBT), only 0.2–0.6 g/day lost and replaced by liver synthesis — the enterohepatic loop is a flyable mini-circuit in its own right.

## 4. What it sounds like and how it is examined

Bowel sounds: normal ~5–35/min (StatPearls NBK459220), clicks and gurgles, unsynchronised with any single peristaltic wave. **[Established limitation — teach it honestly]:** bowel sounds have poor sensitivity/specificity and do not localise; the game should let the student hear them but have rounds reward *not* over-calling them. Succussion splash >3–4 h after a meal is abnormal (gastric outlet delay). Exam sequence matters and is itself examinable: **inspection → auscultation → percussion → palpation** (auscultate before touching, or you manufacture sounds). Percussion: tympany over gas, dullness over fluid/mass; shifting dullness = ascites (liver link); Murphy sign = cholecystitis (biliary link). Outside views: endoscopy (sees mm-scale mucosa only — it does *not* see villi or microvilli; the game's zoom must label each scale jump), FIT (replaced guaiac FOBT for blood), faecal calprotectin (~50 µg/g threshold) separating inflammatory (IBD) from functional (IBS) bowel — inflammation, not cancer.

## 5. Patient case

**Recommendation.** Coeliac disease first: highest system reuse (lacteal failure → the lymph mode's chylomicron route visibly empties; portal route; immune system; iron/B12 anaemia links), the best healthy-vs-disease toggle in all of GI (a villus field flattening is instantly readable), and a *reversible* ending — replay on a gluten-free diet and watch villi regrow. H. pylori ulcer is the short second episode (stomach station, urease, acid). Crohn vs ulcerative colitis are banked as a later paired episode (Crohn: skip lesions, transmural, mouth-to-anus, fistulae; UC: continuous, mucosal, rectum-up, colon only) — teaching them first would spend the whole mode on colon. Obstruction and colorectal screening were compared and parked (obstruction is a mechanics episode; screening is an endoscopy episode).

**Case 03 — "The anaemia that would not stay fixed" (new patient).**
- **Priya Nair, 34, fictional.** Months of bloating and loose stool, fatigue; iron-deficiency anaemia that improves on iron tablets and relapses; low folate; a blistering itchy rash on her elbows (dermatitis herpetiformis, for L4).
- **Bedside/outside first:** pallor, angular stomatitis; bloods (iron, folate, B12 — B12 *preserved*, a localisation clue: duodenum/proximal jejunum, not terminal ileum); anti-tTG IgA strongly positive (with total IgA checked); endoscopy shows scalloped, flat mucosa — then fly in.
- **Inside:** healthy jejunum first (tall villi, villus:crypt 3–5:1). Case toggle: villi blunt and flatten (Marsh 3a→3c), crypts hyperplastic, intraepithelial lymphocytes >25 per 100 enterocytes; fat absorption fails at the lacteal (chylomicron traffic thins — visible in the lymph link), iron uptake at the duodenum falls.
- **Healthy-vs-disease toggle:** `healthy ↔ Marsh 1 (IELs only) ↔ Marsh 3 (flat)` on the same villus field, plus a gluten-free time-lapse regrowth state. Marsh basics: 0 normal · 1 raised IELs · 2 + crypt hyperplasia · 3a/b/c progressive villous atrophy (*Med J Aust* 2017, DOI 10.5694/mja16.00788).
- **Ending variants:** full marks — serology before biopsy, total IgA checked, patient counselled that the diet is the treatment and villi recover over months; partial — diagnosis reached but B12/folate localisation missed; poor — starting a gluten-free diet *before* testing (serology normalises, diagnosis lost) changes the ending, a real clinical trap worth teaching.

## 6. Codex cards

L1 Curious / L2 School / L3 Pre-med / L4 Med school / Reality check. Source: OpenStax A&P 2e + Guyton & Hall / Boron & Boulpaep unless noted.

**The living gut's true length.** L1: Your gut is about as long as you are tall — twice over, not five times. L2: In a living person the whole tube is about 5 metres. L3: Muscle tone shortens the living gut; the 7–9 m figures come from cadavers. L4: In-vivo measurement (Helander & Fändriks 2014) supersedes summed cadaveric segment lengths for counselling and game scale. *RC: This game is built to the living ~5 m, compressed — segment labels keep their anatomy, not cadaver lengths.*

**Surface area.** L1: The gut's inner surface is about the size of a studio flat's floor, not a tennis court. L2: Folds, villi and microvilli multiply the area to ~32 m². L3: Plicae ×~1.6 measured; villi+microvilli ×60–120; the ×600 figure multiplies three textbook maxima. L4: Quote ~30 m² small intestine + ~2 m² colon (Helander & Fändriks 2014) in teaching. *RC: Older books say 260–300 m². The smaller true number still absorbs everything you eat — area was never the limiting factor.*

**The villus fork.** L1: A villus has two drains — one for watery food, one for fat. L2: Sugars and amino acids enter the blood; fat enters the lymph. L3: SGLT1/GLUT transport → portal vein; chylomicrons (apoB-48) → lacteal → thoracic duct. L4: That is why fat-soluble vitamin deficiency and chylous effusions are lymph-pathology, and why portal blood goes to the liver first (first-pass). *RC: "Villi absorb food into the blood" is half the truth — the fat half goes by lymph, which is why the lymph mode's cisterna chyli is milky after a meal.*

**Stomach acid.** L1: Your stomach makes acid stronger than lemon juice — and protects itself from it. L2: Parietal cells pump acid; a mucus layer shields the wall. L3: H/K-ATPase; histamine, gastrin and ACh potentiate each other; secretion pH ~0.9, lumen 1.5–3.5. L4: Defence = mucus-bicarbonate gradient (surface pH ~6–7), prostaglandins, blood flow, rapid renewal; NSAIDs break it by COX-1 inhibition, H. pylori by living in the mucus on urease-generated ammonia. *RC: Acid does not "do the digestion" — it sterilises, denatures and activates pepsin. Most chemical digestion is intestinal, at near-neutral pH.*

**Slow waves & the MMC.** L1: The gut has its own pacemaker rhythm, slower in the stomach, faster in the bowel. L2: Electrical waves pace contractions: stomach 3/min, upper bowel ~12/min. L3: Interstitial cells of Cajal set the frequency; the fasting MMC sweeps the tube every 90–120 min. L4: Phase III is the housekeeper — its absence predisposes to bacterial overgrowth; feeding abolishes the MMC pattern. *RC: Peristalsis is not a constant conveyor — fed and fasting guts run entirely different motor programmes.*

**The microbiome at real scale.** L1: Your colon carries about as many bacteria as you have cells — not ten times more. L2: Bacteria are sparse in the small bowel and dense in the colon. L3: ~3.8×10¹³ bacteria (Sender et al. 2016), density duodenum ~10³/g → ileum ~10⁷/g → colon 10¹¹–10¹²/g; colonocytes burn bacterial butyrate. L4: A sterile inner mucus layer separates epithelium from the colonic mass; its failure is an IBD-relevant lesion. *RC: A game that fills the small bowel with bacteria is wrong by about a hundred-million-fold at the duodenal end.*

**Enteric nervous system & serotonin.** L1: The gut has its own nervous system with hundreds of millions of neurons. L2: Two plexuses run peristalsis without asking the brain. L3: Estimates range ~100–600 million neurons — quote a range; the peristaltic reflex is local (Meissner + Auerbach plexuses). L4: ~90% of body serotonin is made by enterochromaffin cells and acts locally/on platelets — it does not cross the blood–brain barrier, so "gut serotonin is your mood serotonin" is false as stated. *RC: The gut–brain axis is real (vagus, immune, metabolites); the simple serotonin story is the simplification.*

**Bile acids go round.** L1: The liver recycles its soap. L2: Bile salts are reabsorbed in the last part of the small bowel and reused. L3: Pool ~3 g cycling 4–12×/day, ≥95% recovered per pass in the terminal ileum. L4: Ileal disease/resection → bile-acid malabsorption and fat malabsorption; loss is only 0.2–0.6 g/day, matched by synthesis. *RC: Bile is not made fresh for each meal — one pool does many shifts a day.*

**Coeliac disease.** L1: In some people, gluten triggers an attack on their own villi. L2: The villi flatten, so food — especially iron and fat — is poorly absorbed. L3: Villus:crypt falls from 3–5:1 toward flat (Marsh 3), IELs >25/100 enterocytes, anti-tTG IgA positive. L4: Test before a gluten-free diet or serology normalises and the diagnosis is lost; dermatitis herpetiformis is the skin form; villi recover over months on the diet. *RC: "Gluten sensitivity" in the game means this defined immune disease, not a vague intolerance.*

**Appendix.** L1: The appendix is not useless — it is immune tissue, and may be a safe house for good bacteria. L2: It is packed with lymphoid tissue (GALT). L3: A proposed role is re-seeding the colon after diarrhoeal washout (the "safe house" hypothesis — labelled a hypothesis). L4: Appendectomy is still correct treatment for appendicitis; dispensable ≠ functionless. *RC: "No known function" is decades out of date.*

## 7. Rounds questions

Correct answer first.

1. **Q: A glucose molecule absorbed in the jejunum reaches the systemic circulation via…** A: Villus capillary → portal vein → liver first. / The lacteal. / The thoracic duct. / Directly into the aorta. **E:** Water-soluble nutrients take the portal route; only long-chain fat takes lymph — the villus fork.
2. **Q: The best current estimate of total digestive surface area is…** A: ~32 m². / ~260 m² (a tennis court). / ~600 m². / ~7 m². **E:** Helander & Fändriks 2014, direct measurement; the tennis court multiplies textbook maxima.
3. **Q: Bacteria in the duodenum number roughly…** A: ~10³ per gram — the small bowel is sparsely colonised. / 10¹²/g, same as colon. / Zero, it is sterile. / More than in the colon. **E:** The gradient runs 10³ → 10⁷ (ileum) → 10¹¹–10¹² (colon); acid, bile and the MMC keep the upper gut sparse.
4. **Q: Vitamin B12 deficiency after resection localises the lesion to…** A: The terminal ileum (cubam receptor for B12–intrinsic factor). / The duodenum. / The stomach only. / The colon. **E:** Iron is the duodenal one; B12 is ileal — the anaemia pattern localises before imaging.
5. **Q: The fasting housekeeper wave is…** A: MMC phase III, sweeping debris every 90–120 min. / Segmentation. / The gastrocolic reflex. / A slow wave. **E:** Eating abolishes it; slow waves only set the tempo, they do not sweep.
6. **Q: NSAID ulcers arise mainly because…** A: COX-1 inhibition removes prostaglandin defence (mucus, bicarbonate, blood flow). / NSAIDs directly burn the mucosa. / They raise acid tenfold. / They kill parietal cells. **E:** Defence failure, not an acid surge — same logic as H. pylori living in the mucus layer.
7. **Q: Villus:crypt ratio 0:1 with crypt hyperplasia and IELs 40/100 is…** A: Marsh 3 coeliac disease (total villous atrophy). / Normal jejunum. / Crohn disease by definition. / Ulcerative colitis. **E:** Normal is 3–5:1; UC never causes small-bowel villous atrophy.
8. **Q: Which statement about bowel sounds is defensible?** A: They are non-specific and do not localise disease; examine inspection → auscultation → percussion → palpation. / Absent sounds prove ileus. / Hyperactive sounds diagnose obstruction. / They are synchronised to peristalsis you can time. **E:** Poor sensitivity/specificity — hear them, do not over-call them.
9. **Q: Crohn vs ulcerative colitis — the clean discriminator is…** A: Crohn is transmural with skip lesions anywhere mouth-to-anus; UC is mucosal, continuous, colon only from the rectum up. / UC causes fistulae, Crohn does not. / Crohn never affects the colon. / UC causes villous atrophy. **E:** Distribution + depth separate them.
10. **Q: Testing for coeliac disease should happen…** A: While the patient still eats gluten, with anti-tTG IgA plus total IgA. / After 6 months gluten-free. / By HLA typing alone. / Only if villi look flat at endoscopy. **E:** Diet first normalises serology and histology — a lost diagnosis; HLA is a rule-out, not a diagnosis.

## 11. Ranked factual corrections (apply directly)

1. **Surface area 260–300 m² → ~32 m²** (Helander & Fändriks 2014). Any Codex/atlas text quoting a tennis court is wrong.
2. **Gut length 7–9 m → ~5 m living**; segment lengths are cadaveric — do not sum them.
3. **Bacteria outnumber human cells 10:1 → ~1.3:1** (3.8×10¹³ vs 3.0×10¹³; Sender, Fuchs & Milo, *PLoS Biol* 2016, DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1002533).
4. **×600 amplification → plicae ×1.6, villi+microvilli ×60–120.**
5. **"Absorbed into the blood" → two exits:** portal blood for water-soluble, lacteal lymph for long-chain fat.
6. **Small bowel densely colonised → sparse:** 10³/g duodenum vs 10¹¹–10¹²/g colon; keep bacteria off the wall behind the inner mucus layer.
7. **Tongue taste map → wrong** (Hänig 1901 data, Boring's misplot; all regions sense all tastes, thresholds vary slightly).
8. **Appendix "useless" → GALT organ** with a proposed (labelled) safe-house role.
9. **Stomach as main digestion/absorption site → mixer/steriliser/gate**; absorption is small-intestinal, digestion mainly intestinal at near-neutral pH.
10. **Uniform chyme speed → wildly non-uniform:** seconds (oesophagus), hours (stomach/small bowel), 1–2 days (colon).
11. **ENS "500 million neurons" as a fact → a range (~100–600M)**; likewise parietal cells ~1 billion is an estimate.
12. **"Sterile womb" stated flatly → leaning defensible:** placental/fetal microbiome claims are contamination-prone and unproven (Perez-Muñoz et al., *Microbiome* 2017, DOI 10.1186/s40168-017-0268-4) — phrase as "no established resident fetal microbiome."
