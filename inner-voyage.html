An open-world adventure inside a living, medically accurate human — hyper-real to look at and to hear. Fly from mouth to bloodstream to brain, change size to meet cells, bacteria and viruses at their own scale, and learn real biology, microbiology and pathology because the world runs on real medicine. The same heart teaches a 9-year-old “it pumps blood” and a medical student valvular flow, murmurs and heart failure — at different depths, in the same place.

✦ The core fantasy

  • Start outside: you float in a lab, see a whole person, get guided in through the mouth — then the map opens up. Go anywhere.
  • No fake monsters: every creature is a real cell, microbe or molecule with its real job.
  • Size is your superpower: shrink to dodge a white blood cell, grow to ride the bloodstream like a river.
  • The body reacts: fever raises temperature, exercise speeds the heart, a cut triggers an immune swarm.
  • You can hear being inside: the heartbeat is everywhere — loud in the chest, muffled through tissue far away, always keeping time for the whole world.
  • Depth, not age-gating: a kid, a pre-med and a doctor explore the same vessel. What changes is how deep the scans, systems and cases go.

What works: instantly clear, warm, personal, memorable in one second. It also makes a promise — this isn't a body, it's yours — which is exactly the feeling the game wants when the heartbeat speeds up because the host runs.

How learning hides inside play

  • Scan, don't read: press F near anything → 1-line plain-English fact + it joins your Codex. Scan again at a higher depth level for the next layer (see Kid → Doctor).
  • Hear it too: many scans unlock a real sound — listen to a normal valve, then a stenotic one, until you can tell them apart.
  • Learn by needing it: you can't pass a WBC checkpoint until you learn what an antigen badge is — by earning one.
  • Missions = real jobs: deliver O₂ before a muscle cell fades, escort insulin, unblock a tiny clot with Platelets.

07 · Hyper-realism — the new north star

Real anatomy first. Beauty from truth, not gore.

“Hyper-realistic” here does not mean horror-movie wet and red everywhere. It means: proportions, structures, materials and behaviour are medically true, rendered as convincingly as current tech allows — translucent vessel walls with real branching, RBCs that actually deform to squeeze through capillaries, lungs inflating with real mechanics. When a player learns what something looks like, they should be learning the real thing.

👁 Visual realism standard

  • Anatomy: built / checked against standard references (Netter atlas, OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology, Visible Body-style 3D models). Every organ, vessel route and valve in the right place, at relative scale.
  • Cell biology: electron-microscope truth at Scale 4–5 — rough ER really is studded, mitochondria have folded cristae, viruses have correct spike geometry (influenza ≠ coronavirus ≠ bacteriophage).
  • Materials: wet specular tissue, subsurface-scattered skin/vessel walls, volumetric “plasma haze”, soft bodies that pulse, stretch and recoil. Light comes from your ship + bioluminescent-style instrument glow — no fake daylight inside a body.
  • Physiology you can see: vessel walls pulse with systole, alveoli expand/contract, gut peristalsis is a travelling wave, pupils, peristalsis, cilia beating in waves (metachronal waves in airways).
  • Pathology looks different: inflamed tissue is genuinely swollen/redder/hotter-looking, atherosclerotic plaque narrows a vessel asymmetrically, infected cells bulge with virions. You diagnose partly by looking — like a real clinician.

⚠ The honest trade-off (important)

Full photorealism across a whole body is a AAA-studio, multi-year effort — it is not a solo v1. So the plan uses tiered realism:

  • Hero realism where the player is looking: current biome, creatures, vessel walls — highest fidelity.
  • Stylised-real distance: far organs read as true anatomy with simpler shaders (still correct shapes/places).
  • Never fake the science to save polygons: simplify rendering, never simplify what is true. A low-poly alveolus is fine; oxygen diffusing the wrong way is not.
  • Engine implication: this pushes the project toward Unreal Engine 5 (Nanite / Lumen, MetaSounds — see audio) over Godot for the final game. Prototype gameplay cheaply anywhere; commit to UE5 for the realism target.
  • Comfort & rating: realism without gore — no gratuitous blood-splatter, surgical or horror framing. Target: awe, not disgust. This keeps it kid-safe and clinically respectable.

🩺 Headline feature · locked

STETHOSCOPE MODE

At any moment, hold [H] (or the on-screen 🩺) and the game changes state: camera steadies, ship noise fades, HUD minimises, and listening becomes the mechanic. This is the feature med students will screenshot and share — auscultation taught by actually auscultating, in 3D, on a living patient who breathes, moves and deteriorates.

Real auscultation, real landmarks

  • Cardiac: aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral areas — plus Erb's point. You must travel inside to the valve or place the scope correctly on the chest map; proximity + angle change what you hear, as in life.
  • Respiratory: anterior/posterior fields, side-to-side comparison (the game rewards comparing left vs right, like you're taught).
  • Bowel & vascular: quadrants, bruits over narrowed arteries, Doppler-style flow mode at higher levels.
  • Bell vs diaphragm: toggle changes frequency emphasis — low rumbles (S3, mitral stenosis) vs high (aortic regurgitation, breath sounds). A real distinction, made audible.

How it teaches (L4 lead, L1 accessible)

  • Listen → see: simultaneous phonocardiogram / airflow waveform, synced to the valve or alveoli you're inside. Sound becomes visible, then the visual fades as you improve.
  • Library & compare: A/B normal vs murmur, crackles vs wheeze, present vs absent bowel sounds — from cited medical sound libraries, labelled, never mystery audio.
  • Case integration: “You hear a diastolic murmur at the left sternal edge” is something you did, so the differential (and the OSCE-style Rounds question) is earned.
  • Accessibility: waveform + captions + haptic beat always available. Stethoscope Mode rewards listening; it never punishes hearing differences.

How the dynamic mix works

  • One heartbeat, everywhere: a single cardiac clock (e.g. 72 bpm at rest) drives a low “lub-dub” heard across the map — loud & sharp in the heart, a muffled thump through tissue in a fingertip, felt as a pulse-surge in arteries. Distance + tissue type = volume, low-pass filter and delay.
  • Blood flow is positional: whoosh synced to systole near arteries, steady river-hiss in veins, near-silence + squeeze-creaks in capillaries. Turbulence = a clue: a narrowed valve sounds different (a real murmur).
  • Breathing is weather: ~12–16 breaths/min at rest drive lung wind, airway whistle, and chest rise. Near the diaphragm you get pushed by the tide.
  • Local biomes add their voice: gut borborygmi (real gurgles, peristalsis pops), stomach churn, joint creaks, neural “electrical hum” is not faked — brain zones stay eerily quiet except blood pulse, which teaches that neurons are electrical, not noisy.
  • State changes are heard first: exercise → rate & breath climb; fever → faster, restless gut; sleep → slow, deep, regular; fear/pain → spikes. Players learn to listen diagnostically — stethoscope thinking, without a stethoscope.

Sound design rules

  • Source truth: start from real recordings where possible (heart/lung/bowel sound libraries used in medical training, Doppler flow) + physically-modelled synthesis for scale-shifts. At viral scale, “sound” becomes low vibration — the mix gets sub-bass and tactile (controller rumble = heartbeat).
  • Spatial tech: UE5 MetaSounds / Wwise, HRTF binaural for headphones, tissue occlusion (bone conducts, air muffles, fluid carries).
  • Accessibility: visual heartbeat waveform + optional captions (“distant heartbeat, faster than before”) and a reduced-intensity mode. Audio is a learning channel, never the only channel.
  • Silence is information: absent bowel sounds, absent breath sound on one side — at clinical levels, missing sounds become diagnostic puzzles.
  • No jump-scare audio. Even immune alarms are biological (a rush of flow, a temperature shimmer), not horror stingers.
09 · One world, five depths of medicine

From a kid's first “wow” to a doctor's differential.

Not separate games and not age-locked doors. It's one body with five layers of truth, unlocked by curiosity, scans and completed cases — exactly how real medical training stacks: basic science first, then systems, then pathology, then clinical reasoning. v1 design centre = Level 4 (medical students). Levels 1–3 are the onboarding/revision ramp, Level 5 is the stretch goal. A player chooses (and can change) their depth; the world never talks down to them, it just reveals more.

🔬 How depth works mechanically

  • Layered Codex: every scan has stacked cards. RBC at L1: “carries oxygen”. L2: haemoglobin & biconcave shape. L3: gas laws, Hb–O₂ dissociation. L4: anaemia types. L5: a case — fatigue + pallor, what do you check? Same creature, deeper truth each return visit.
  • Layered systems: higher levels switch on invisible systems you can now perceive — membrane potentials, hormone gradients, immune signalling (cytokines as scent-trails), pharmacology (a drug you deliver has dose, route, half-life).
  • Layered failure: at L1 a virus is “stop the copies”. At L4 you must consider viral life-cycle stage, interferon response and why antibiotics do nothing. At L5 you manage a deteriorating patient over time.
  • Assessment without exams (unless you want them): mastery shown by doing — a correct delivery, a correct diagnosis. Optional “Rounds Mode” at L4–L5 gives OSCE-style cases and references for students who want to test themselves.

📚 Curriculum spine (mapped to real training)

  • Before med school (L1–L3): cells & organelles, DNA→protein, diffusion/osmosis, basic anatomy, physiology, intro microbiology, biochemistry of metabolism.
  • Med school basic sciences (L4): systems physiology, pathology (inflammation, neoplasia, cell injury), pharmacology, microbiology & immunology in depth.
  • Clinical (L5): history-by-exploration (“interview the organ”), signs you can see/hear in-world, investigations (order a scan of the scan — labs as mini-reports), differential diagnosis, management & follow-up.
  • Accuracy process: every fact/case needs a named source + clinician/medical-educator review pass before it ships. Build a small medical advisory board early — even 2–3 reviewers changes everything.

Suggested refs to anchor content: OpenStax A&P, TeachMeAnatomy, Osmosis, AMBOSS-style case logic (as model, not copied), First Aid (structure only).

10 · Build plan — start embarrassingly small, aim medically big

Don't build the whole body first. Build one great, true vessel.

1
Prototype (2–4 weeks) — “One Blood Vessel, heard properly”

Third-person ship in a single looping vessel. RBCs deform past you, one WBC patrols, shrink/grow Scale 2–3 only, 5 layered Codex cards. Plus the proof-of-concept for this whole vision: one heartbeat heard everywhere, louder/filtered by distance, driving the pulse in the wall. If this isn't fun — and doesn't sound like being inside — in 3 minutes, nothing else matters. UE5 locked. Playtest target from day one: medical students.

2
Vertical slice (2–3 months) — Mouth → Heart, Level 4 core (L1–2 as onboarding)

The guided intro: float in, enter mouth, ride down, reach the heart. Body map (M), one delivery mission, dynamic heart/breath audio states (rest → exercise), anatomically checked route. First real auscultation case: reach a valve, enter Stethoscope Mode, distinguish normal S1/S2 from one murmur. Playtest with medical students; you sign off every sound label and Codex layer as medical accuracy lead.

3
Three biomes + pathology (term project)

Add Lungs & Gut, Scale 4 (viruses), Platelet roadworks, first pathology states (inflammation, a narrowed vessel you can hear), Level 3 (pre-med) Codex layers. Recruit medical reviewers; start the source-citation habit for every claim.

4
Clinical layer (only if the core is beloved)

Level 4–5: cases, Rounds Mode, drug delivery with dose/route, deteriorating-patient arcs, other playable characters, molecular scale. This is where it becomes a tool med schools could pilot — with formal accuracy review and learning-outcome playtests.

Art direction — REVISED in v0.2

Hyper-real anatomy, humane presentation. This replaces the earlier cheap-stylised advice. Correct structures, wet/translucent materials, real motion (pulse, peristalsis, cilia), pathology you can see and hear — rendered in UE5 with tiered fidelity (hero-real near you, simpler at distance). No gore, no horror. Reference: medical illustration + electron microscopy + endoscopy footage, art-directed for wonder.

Scope guardrails (now even more important)

  • Realism budget goes to one biome at a time — never spread thin across 9 half-true organs
  • One host body, not customisable humans (yet)
  • No combat-first design — outsmart, escort, deliver, repair, diagnose
  • Every fact/case: named source + clinical review before shipping
  • Audio and visual state come from one physiology simulation (heart rate, breath, temperature, inflammation) — don't fake them per-room
  • If a system doesn't teach, delight, or sound/look true, cut it
