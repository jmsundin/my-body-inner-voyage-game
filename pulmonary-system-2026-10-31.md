Draft for Jon's review as medical accuracy lead. Flags: **[Established]** · **[Debated]** · **[Revised]** · **[Wrong]** commonly taught, not true.*

**Verdict up front.** The pulmonary mode is the mirror of the circulation: the player has already crossed the alveolus from the *blood* side — now they arrive from the *air* side and meet the same red cells through a barrier less than a micrometre thick. Build it as a one-way inhalation dive (air in), with exhalation as a genuine ejection hazard, and the elegant exit the brief's logic demands: **air in, blood out** — dock in an alveolus, transfer to a red cell, and leave by the pulmonary vein on the existing blood loop, retracing oxygen's own path. The new clock is breathing (~5 s cycle), coupled to — never replacing — the cardiac clock.

**Headline numbers.** Trachea Ø ~18 mm, 10–12 cm, 16–20 C-rings; right main bronchus wider, ~2.5 cm, ~25° (foreign bodies go right) vs left ~5 cm, ~45°. Weibel generations 0–23: diameter 1.8 cm → 0.041 cm, total cross-section 2.5 cm² → ~180 cm² (gen 16) → ~11,800 cm² (gen 23) — flow slows to near-stillness, which is the point. Conducting zone gens 0–16 ≈ 150 mL = anatomical dead space; respiratory zone 17–23. Alveoli mean ~480 million (range 274–790M; bigger lungs have *more*, not bigger), ~250 µm, polyhedral with shared septa. Capillary transit ~0.75 s at rest, gases equilibrate by ~0.25 s; at maximal exercise transit falls to ~0.25 s. Mean pulmonary artery pressure ~14–15 mmHg — a tenth of systemic resistance. Overall V/Q 0.8; apex 3.3, base 0.6.

**Recommended first case: lobar pneumonia. Short second episode: cardiogenic pulmonary oedema — in Mr. Reyes.** Reasoning in §5.

Air-side tube, branching — the game's first tree rather than loop. ## 2. What it looks like, station by station

**Upper airway.** Turbinates as scroll shelves maximising contact; respiratory epithelium (pseudostratified ciliated columnar + goblet cells) from nose to bronchioles. *Microscopy:* SEM ciliated lawn (~200 cilia/cell, 5–6 µm); LM wall-layer stack in bronchi (epithelium, smooth muscle, cartilage plates — plates, not rings, below the trachea).

**The escalator.** **[Revised]** The periciliary layer is not a watery "sol": Button et al. (*Science* 2012, DOI 10.1126/science.1223012) showed it is a tethered-mucin *brush* (20–40 nm mesh) that mucus gel rides on — gel-on-brush. Cilia beat 10–15 Hz; mucus moves 0.5–1 mm/min in small airways, 5–20 mm/min in the trachea. CFTR secretes Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻, ENaC absorbs Na⁺ — hydration is a tug-of-war the player can watch fail in disease (osmotic collapse of the brush in CF/COPD). Club (ex-Clara) cells in bronchioles: secretory, detox, progenitor. Cough as the backup system: glottic closure, intrathoracic pressure to ~300 mmHg, flow >500 L/min, effective only to ~gen 7–8 — below that, the escalator is the only way out. **[Wrong]** Airways are never empty dry pipes; there is always a moving mucus surface under the sub.

**Alveolus.** **[Wrong] → correction:** not a bunch of grapes. Alveoli are polyhedral air spaces sharing septa like a foam, each with 7–9 neighbours; walls are a *sheet-like capillary network* (not a single loop — Mühlfeld et al. 2015, DOI 10.1152/ajplung.00047.2015), entrance rings of elastin/collagen, pores of Kohn between neighbours. Type I pneumocytes: ~95% of surface, a minority of cells. Type II: roughly twice as numerous, cuboidal, lamellar bodies on TEM, make surfactant *and* serve as progenitors. Alveolar macrophages patrol the air surface. **[Debated]** Surface area: older texts ~70 m² vs EM stereology 120–140 m² — method- and inflation-dependent; the Codex should quote ~100 m² ±40% with the Reality-check saying exactly that, rather than a false precision.

**Surfactant & Laplace.** Surfactant ~85–90% lipid (DPPC the main film species; the exact % depends on the denominator — flagged in research), SP-B/C work the film, SP-A/D are immune collectins. The Laplace problem (P = 2γ/r: small alveoli should empty into big ones) is solved three ways, all showable: surface tension *falls* as the film compresses at small radius; alveoli hold each other up (interdependence of the shared foam); pores of Kohn equalise. **[Wrong]** Healthy alveoli are not balloons being recruited breath by breath.

**The barrier.** Three layers fused on the thin side: type I epithelium + fused basement membrane + capillary endothelium, 0.2–0.6 µm; the thick side carries interstitium (fluid exchange, support) — roughly half thin, half thick along the septum. This asymmetry becomes gameplay in the oedema episode: flooding starts on the thick side.

## 3. What moves and on what clock

**New clock: breathing.** Rest: rate ~12/min, tidal volume ~500 mL, dead space ~150 mL, minute ventilation ~6.0 L/min, alveolar ventilation ~4.2 L/min — the dead-space subtraction ((500−150)×12) is a Codex worked sum. Diaphragm does ~75% of the work, intercostals ~25%. Pressures: pleural −5 cmH₂O at FRC, −7.5 on inspiration; alveolar −1/+1; transpulmonary +5 at FRC. There is **no air gap** between lung and chest wall — pleural fluid only; the "pleural space" as an empty gap is a **[Wrong]** diagram habit. Compliance: lung ~200, chest wall ~200, combined ~100–110 mL/cmH₂O; hysteresis comes from surfactant; work of breathing only ~1–3% of VO₂ at rest — which is why its rise in disease is so costly.

**Coupling to the cardiac clock (the brief's §3.4 test):** one breath ≈ 5 s ≈ 6 heartbeats. Inspiration raises venous return (respiratory pump) and heart rate (respiratory sinus arrhythmia); systolic BP normally dips <10 mmHg on inspiration, >10 = pulsus paradoxus. I:E ~1:2, lengthening in obstruction — an audible, playable rhythm change. Exercise couples both clocks up together, and capillary transit time becomes the shared constraint (Case 01 already teaches it from the blood side).

**Pulmonary circulation intricacies (the showcase list):**
- **Low-pressure design:** PA ~25/8, mean ~14–15 mmHg; PVR ~a tenth of systemic. **[Revised]** Pulmonary hypertension is now defined at mean PAP >20 mmHg (2022 ESC/ERS), not 25.
- **West zones 1–3 (+4)** are *pressure regimes that move* with posture, volume and pressure — not anatomy. Gravity makes apex and base different organs, functionally.
- **V/Q:** overall 0.8, apex 3.3 (over-ventilated), base 0.6 (over-perfused). The two failure extremes are the game's two pulmonary villains: **dead space** (ventilated, not perfused — pulmonary embolism) vs **shunt** (perfused, not ventilated — pneumonia/atelectasis). A–a gradient ~5–15 mmHg young, ≈ age/4 + 4.
- **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction** (Euler–Liljestrand): alveolar (not arterial) PO₂ is sensed; local vessels constrict and divert blood to ventilated units. Brilliant locally, catastrophic when global (altitude, diffuse lung disease → pulmonary hypertension). Show it as a valve the player can watch close at a flooded alveolus — and fail to save them in pneumonia (§5).
- **Bronchial circulation:** a second, systemic-pressure supply (~1–2% of cardiac output) — the source of most haemoptysis and part of the anatomical shunt.
- **Endothelium as an organ:** ACE conversion, mediator inactivation, barrier — the capillary wall is working, not wallpaper.
- **Control of breathing:** CO₂, via CSF H⁺ at central chemoreceptors, provides ~70–80% of the drive — that is *why* CO₂ and not O₂ is the normal driver. Carotid bodies sense PO₂ (not content) and fire steeply below ~60 mmHg. P50 ~26.6 mmHg; Bohr (CO₂/H⁺ unloads O₂ in tissues) and Haldane (deoxygenation carries more CO₂) are reciprocals — one diagram, both directions, at the P9 barrier. CO₂ transport ~70% bicarbonate / ~20–23% carbamino / ~7–10% dissolved. **[Wrong — do not teach]** the COPD "hypoxic drive" story as a reason to withhold oxygen; it is an oversimplified myth. Also flagged: pneumotaxic/apneustic "centres" are a historical box model; Hering–Breuer's threshold sits above normal tidal volume in awake adults.

## 4. What it sounds like and how it is examined

All synthesisable (Bohadana et al., *NEJM* 2014, DOI 10.1056/NEJMra1302901 — spectrogram characterisations): **vesicular** = low-pass-filtered noise (~100–1,000 Hz, energy dropping ~200 Hz) — **[Wrong]** it is not made by alveoli; it is bronchial noise filtered by lung. **Tracheal/bronchial** ~100–5,000 Hz unfiltered; bronchial breathing heard over consolidation is unfiltered sound arriving where it shouldn't. **Wheeze:** sinusoid >80 ms, pitch rising with narrowing. **Fine crackles** ~650 Hz, ~5 ms, late-inspiratory — small-airway *reopening pops*, not bubbles through fluid; **coarse** ~350 Hz = secretions. **Stridor** >500 Hz, inspiratory, over the neck (upper airway — a different emergency from wheeze). **Pleural rub:** creaking, biphasic. Percussion: resonant / hyperresonant (pneumothorax, emphysema) / dull (consolidation) / stony dull (effusion). Vocal resonance cluster — bronchophony, whispered pectoriloquy, egophony (E→A) — sorts consolidation vs effusion vs pneumothorax at the bedside. Outside views: spirometry (FEV1/FVC >0.70 normal, obstructive <0.70, restrictive = low volumes with preserved/high ratio; the fixed 0.70 cutoff vs lower-limit-of-normal is **[Debated]** — label it), pulse oximetry *with its traps* (it reads saturation, not ventilation; a normal SpO₂ can hide rising CO₂).

## 5. Patient case

**Recommendation.** Asthma, COPD/emphysema, lobar pneumonia, pulmonary embolism and ARDS/oedema were compared on teaching value, reuse of built systems, toggle payoff and bedside hooks. **Lobar pneumonia wins as the first pulmonary case** because it is the only one that uses *everything already built*: the alveolar capillary (blood side), the air tree (new), the lymph/immune set (neutrophils, macrophages), and it teaches the single most examinable pulmonary concept — **shunt**: alveoli filled with exudate are perfused but not ventilated, HPV tries and only partly compensates (shunt fraction ~10–30%), the A–a gradient widens, and hypoxaemia responds only partly to oxygen (true shunt physiology, distinguishable from V/Q scatter at the bedside of the game). Its level arc is the pathology's own: red hepatisation → grey hepatisation → resolution, with the alveolar foam flooding and then clearing. **Second, short episode: cardiogenic pulmonary oedema in Mr. Reyes** — it pays off Case 01's failing heart (wedge pressure ~18–25 mmHg vs oncotic ~25–28; interstitial then alveolar flooding, thick side first; heart-failure cells; fine crackles and orthopnoea), and sets up ARDS later (Berlin criteria PaO₂/FiO₂ 300/200/100 on PEEP ≥5). Banked with numbers ready: asthma (reversibility ≥12% + ≥200 mL, DLCO normal — a plumbing, not membrane, disease), emphysema (septal destruction, DLCO *falls*, compliance *rises* — the opposite membrane lesion), PE (dead space, VD/VT >0.5, RV pressure ceiling ~40 mmHg mean).

**Case 04 — "The cough that turned to rust" (new patient).**
- **Tomás Herrera, 68, fictional.** Three days of fever and cough, now breathless at rest, pleuritic pain, rusty sputum; a lifetime non-smoker (keeps COPD out of the differential the game is teaching).
- **Bedside first (the full consolidation cluster, all findable):** fever, tachypnoea, SpO₂ low with a wide A–a on the gas; over the right lower lobe — dull percussion, bronchial breathing, increased vocal resonance with egophony, coarse-to-fine crackles. The student localises the lobe *before* flying in.
- **Inside:** air route to the affected acinus — alveoli filled with neutrophil-rich exudate (red hepatisation: red cells, neutrophils, fibrin), the capillary beside them still flowing (that juxtaposition *is* shunt — show PO₂ of blood leaving the unit barely risen); watch HPV constrict the feeding arteriole and only partly divert flow. Healthy-vs-disease toggle at P8/P9: `ventilated ↔ consolidated ↔ resolving` (grey hepatisation → macrophage clearance in resolution).
- **Endings:** full marks — shunt named, partial O₂ response predicted, lobe localised at the bedside; partial — pneumonia diagnosed but V/Q mechanism missed; poor — calling it dead space/PE physiology (the mirror error) reroutes the ending to a PE work-up conversation.

## 6. Codex cards

L1–L4 + Reality check. Source: OpenStax A&P 2e + West's *Respiratory Physiology* unless noted.

**The tree that slows the wind.** L1: Your airways branch ~23 times, and the air slows to almost still. L2: Each branch halves the tubes' size but multiplies their number, so total width explodes. L3: Cross-section 2.5 cm² (trachea) → ~11,800 cm² (gen 23); beyond gen ~16, diffusion, not flow, moves gas. L4: That crossover is why inhaled drug particle size decides deposition site, and why small-airway disease is silent until advanced. *RC: Real lungs are asymmetric — paths run 8–24 generations. The symmetric Weibel tree is a model, drawn as one chosen path.*

**Dead space.** L1: The first part of every breath never reaches the gas-exchange surface. L2: ~150 mL fills the conducting airways (gens 0–16). L3: Alveolar ventilation = (tidal volume − dead space) × rate = (500−150)×12 ≈ 4.2 L/min. L4: Added dead space (PE) wastes ventilation; VD/VT >0.5 is severe. *RC: Rapid shallow breathing can move plenty of air and ventilate almost nothing — run the numbers.*

**The alveolus.** L1: Half a billion tiny air pockets share their walls like a foam. L2: ~480 million alveoli, ~250 µm each, wrapped in capillary sheets. L3: More alveoli in bigger lungs, not bigger ones (Ochs et al. 2004); pores of Kohn connect neighbours. L4: Surface ~100 m² ±40% by method — quote the range; septal capillary network, not loops. *RC: Not grapes on stalks — cut a grape bunch and it stays whole; cut this foam and every wall is shared.*

**Surfactant.** L1: Soap for lungs — it stops small air pockets collapsing into big ones. L2: Type II cells secrete surfactant, which lowers surface tension more as the alveolus shrinks. L3: DPPC film + SP-B/C; dynamic compression makes γ fall with radius, defeating Laplace (P = 2γ/r), aided by interdependence. L4: Surfactant failure = neonatal RDS and a driver of ARDS mechanics; type II cells are also the progenitors that repair the alveolus. *RC: Healthy alveoli do not pop open each breath — the film works continuously.*

**The barrier.** L1: Air and blood pass within a hair's breadth — a 500th of one. L2: The blood–gas barrier is three fused layers, under a micrometre thick. L3: Type I cell + fused basement membrane + endothelium; thin side 0.2–0.6 µm for exchange, thick side for fluid balance. L4: DLCO (~25 mL/min/mmHg) tests this membrane + capillary volume together — it falls in emphysema/fibrosis, not in pure asthma. *RC: Gases cross the barrier; the sub never does. This station is a window, not a door.*

**V/Q matching.** L1: Air and blood must arrive at the same place, in the right proportions. L2: Overall V/Q ≈ 0.8; the apex is air-rich (3.3), the base blood-rich (0.6). L3: Gravity + West zones set the gradient; HPV diverts blood from unventilated units. L4: The extremes name the diseases: shunt V/Q = 0 (pneumonia) vs dead space V/Q → ∞ (PE); A–a gradient separates them from hypoventilation. *RC: Zones are pressure regimes that move when you lie the patient down — the game re-computes them, it does not repaint anatomy.*

**Breathing is a CO₂ sense.** L1: You breathe to blow off carbon dioxide, not to fetch oxygen. L2: Brain sensors read CO₂ (as acid) and set the rate. L3: Central chemoreceptors give ~70–80% of the CO₂ response; carotid bodies answer O₂ only below ~60 mmHg. L4: Hence hypercapnia drives ventilation until chronic retention adapts it — and the "hypoxic drive" reason to withhold O₂ in COPD is a myth; give oxygen, monitor CO₂. *RC: A normal oxygen reading can hide dangerous CO₂ retention. Saturation is not ventilation.*

**Breath sounds.** L1: Chest sounds are filtered noise, and the filter is the lung itself. L2: Vesicular = soft, filtered; bronchial = harsh, unfiltered, abnormal away from the trachea. L3: Wheeze is a musical sinusoid from a narrowing tube; fine crackles are reopening pops (~650 Hz, late inspiration). L4: Sound maps to mechanism: consolidation transmits voice (egophony), effusion blocks it, pneumothorax silences it. *RC: "Vesicular" sounds are not made by alveoli (vesicles) — the name is a historical error the game keeps but corrects.*

## 7. Rounds questions

Correct answer first.

1. **Q: Blood leaving a consolidated, airless lobe has a low PO₂ mainly because of…** A: Shunt — perfusion of unventilated alveoli that HPV only partly diverts. / Dead space. / Hypoventilation alone. / A thick barrier. **E:** Perfused-but-not-ventilated is V/Q = 0; that is also why the hypoxaemia only partly corrects with oxygen.
2. **Q: Foreign bodies aspirated by adults most often enter…** A: The right main bronchus — wider and more vertical (~25°). / The left. / Either equally. / The trachea and stay there. **E:** Anatomy decides; the game makes you learn it by drifting right yourself.
3. **Q: Alveolar ventilation at rest (VT 500, dead space 150, rate 12) is…** A: (500−150)×12 ≈ 4.2 L/min. / 6.0 L/min. / 150×12. / 500×12 minus nothing. **E:** 6.0 L/min is minute ventilation; the first 150 mL of each breath never reaches alveoli.
4. **Q: Mean pulmonary artery pressure is normally…** A: ~14–15 mmHg — a low-pressure circuit with ~a tenth of systemic resistance. / ~90 mmHg. / ~120/80. / Equal to wedge pressure. **E:** And PH is now defined from >20 mmHg (2022 ESC/ERS), not 25.
5. **Q: Small alveoli do not empty into large ones because…** A: Surfactant's surface tension falls as radius falls, plus alveolar interdependence. / Air cannot move between alveoli. / Large alveoli have higher pressure. / Cartilage holds them open. **E:** Laplace alone predicts collapse; dynamic surfactant + shared walls defeat it — pores of Kohn actually do connect neighbours.
6. **Q: Egophony and bronchial breathing over a dull area indicate…** A: Consolidation transmitting unfiltered sound. / Pleural effusion. / Pneumothorax. / Normal lung. **E:** Effusion dulls too but *blocks* voice sounds — the pairing, not one sign, localises.
7. **Q: The normal driver of ventilation is…** A: CO₂ sensed as H⁺ by central chemoreceptors. / Oxygen at the carotid body. / Lung stretch (Hering–Breuer) at normal volumes. / Voluntary control. **E:** Carotid O₂ sensing is the emergency back-up below ~60 mmHg; Hering–Breuer's threshold exceeds normal VT in awake adults.
8. **Q: DLCO is normal in pure asthma but falls in emphysema because…** A: Asthma narrows airways without destroying the exchange membrane; emphysema destroys septa and capillary surface. / Asthma patients are younger. / DLCO only measures airways. / Emphysema raises compliance so gas cannot diffuse. **E:** Resistance vs membrane — the spirometry/DLCO pairing separates them.
9. **Q: At maximal exercise, pulmonary capillary transit time…** A: Falls to ~0.25 s — just at the equilibration limit, so diffusion limitation can appear. / Rises to 2 s. / Stays 0.75 s regardless. / Becomes irrelevant. **E:** Rest has a 3× safety margin (0.75 vs 0.25 s); exercise spends it — Case 01's stairs from the air side.
10. **Q: Fine crackles are generated by…** A: Explosive reopening of small airways in late inspiration. / Bubbles through mucus. / Alveoli filling with fluid. / The pleura rubbing. **E:** Coarse crackles are the secretions sound; rub is biphasic and creaking — three mechanisms, three sounds.

## 8. Objectives

In play order:

1. **Bedside:** examine Mr. Herrera — find the consolidation cluster (dullness, bronchial breathing, egophony) and localise the lobe.
2. **Inhale:** ride one breath from the nose; survive the carina without taking the right-bias trap unawares (then do — once — to learn it).
3. **Escalator watch:** at a bronchiole, observe mucus transport against your direction of travel; note the brush, not water, under it.
4. **Feel the flow die:** cross gen ~16 and switch to diffusion drift; read the scale bar change.
5. **Dock at a septum (P9):** watch a red cell load O₂ in ~0.25 s through the thin side; identify type I, type II and a macrophage.
6. **Bohr/Haldane turn:** ride the same red cell's CO₂ unloading, then exit by the pulmonary vein into the known loop (air in, blood out).
7. **Zone shift:** stand the patient up, then lie them down — watch West zones and V/Q at your acinus change.
8. **Case toggle on:** flood the acinus (consolidation); measure outflow PO₂ barely rising; watch HPV constrict and only partly rescue it.
9. **Oxygen trial:** raise FiO₂ and see the partial response — prove shunt, not dead space.
10. **Present on rounds:** §7 set, patient-course meter running; resolution time-lapse as the ending.
